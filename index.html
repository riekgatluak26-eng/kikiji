Who We Are

An organization built around children, not around paperwork.

Kikiji National Orphanage Centre – South Sudan was legally established in 2020 as a nonpolitical, nonprofit national charity organization, registered with the Government of South Sudan in Juba under Registration Number 3626. The orphanage is governed and managed by strong voluntary board members and an executive body.

We are currently based in Yei River County, Central Equatoria State. Through individual resource mobilization support, KNOC-SS provides education sponsorship to orphans and other vulnerable children through paying their tuition, scholastic materials, and stationery. We also provide counseling, awareness on child protection through the media, psychosocial support to children through acrobatics training, child protection case management, and child protection training to caretakers.

The orphanage is an equal opportunity charity organization; our selection criteria do not discriminate on tribe, gender, and religion. For instance, the current children benefiting from the program are from 15 different tribes across South Sudan. Our primary focus is on orphans and vulnerable children to ensure that no child suffers or perishes from abuse, neglect or deprivation and that every child grows up in a loving center and has access to education.

Our Objectives

The goals that guide every decision we make.

Six commitments we hold ourselves to — for the child, the family, and the community around them.

Six commitments
  1. 01

    To provide emergency protective care, a transitional orphanage centre and community reintegration services for orphans and other vulnerable children.

  2. 02

    To provide a safe shelter and family-like environment for orphans and other vulnerable children who have no one else to take care of them.

  3. 03

    To provide children under our care with adequate diet, clothing, access to safe healthcare, age-appropriate education and guidance, while protecting them from neglect, discrimination, violence and abuse.

  4. 04

    To endeavor to reconnect with existing loving family members that can provide proper care for the children. Where a child is unable to return to his or her parents or extended family, to connect children under our care with loving foster and adoptive parents.

  5. 05

    To reduce vulnerability by providing support for caretakers and the families of vulnerable children in fields such as education, health, family planning and resourcefulness.

  6. 06

    To protect each child's rights to heritage, religion and tribal linkage.

What We Offer

Programs that meet children where they are.

Our work is organized around the core program areas below — each one designed to address a different part of a child's life.

01

Education

Our intention is to provide education and career opportunities to orphaned, deprived and destitute children. Our immediate support is to keep such children in school and ensure the children continue accessing education like other children until they attain a level where they take the knowledge and skills to stand on their own.

  • Sponsorship: Financial support for tuition, scholastic materials, and stationery for orphans and vulnerable children.
  • Career Guidance & Counseling: Career guidance, counseling, and leadership training for school-going children to equip them with essential skills.

02

Psychosocial Support

Trauma healing, counseling, feeding program and family link/rehabilitation.

  • Counseling & Mentorship: Provision of counseling and mentorship to address emotional and psychological challenges.
  • Trauma Healing: Trauma healing through acrobatics training within Child-Friendly Spaces (CFS), fostering emotional recovery.

03

Advocacy

KNOC believes that 'No child should grow up alone'. Our advocacy work is designed to change policy and practice to improve national child and family welfare systems in South Sudan. Awareness is carried through radio stations, open markets, newspapers, and social media, making policies that protect children and organizing seminars and conferences to train parents and caretakers on child protection.

Five focus areas

Policy influence Capacity building Evidence & research Partnerships & networking Media engagements

04

Family Strengthening

In our Family Strengthening program, our desired results are targeted at a child, family, and the community. The design of our FS services consists of a combination of two approaches: direct family empowerment (DFE) and community empowerment for family empowerment (CFE). Both approaches work towards the shared aim of family empowerment.

While working with families and communities we aim to achieve self-reliance families and sustainability of communities through a range of services such as:

Livelihood support Parenting workshops Psychological counselling Entrepreneurship training Vocational training

05

Safety and Security

  • Safe Housing: Protection through safe housing solutions for orphans, ensuring a secure living environment.
  • Basic Necessities: Provision of essential housing necessities to support the wellbeing of the children.
  • Health Care Services: Comprehensive healthcare, including mental health services, to address the medical needs of vulnerable children.

06

Talent Identification & Development

Through acrobatics and gymnastics training, this project has helped children from Yei, South Sudan identify and develop their talents. The program has united children, healed them of trauma, promoted talent, and engaged idle children in productive activities.

It has helped create a message of peace, unity and harmony between the children of different backgrounds and tribes under the orphanage by training together.

What has changed

Bicycle Initiative

Getting to school, safely and on time.

  • Helped our children arrive at school on time and reduced absenteeism.
  • Reduced protection risks faced mainly by our female learners, caused by the long distance to and from school.
  • Reduced the number of harassment incidents toward our girls on the way to school.
  • Those who received bicycles now have full attendance of class and good performance.
School Feeding Program

A meal that makes room for learning.

  • Improved the children's nutrition and overall health.
  • Helped raise attendance across the center.
  • Increased enrolment of children to school.
  • Helped children understand their lessons better and perform well.
Acrobatics & Gymnastics

Talent, healing, and peace in the same room.

This project has helped children from Yei, South Sudan identify and develop their talents in acrobatics and gymnastic skills. The program has united children, healed them of trauma, promoted talent, and engaged idle children in productive activities.

It has helped create a message of peace, unity and harmony between the children of different backgrounds and tribes under the orphanage by training together.

State House · Juba 18 March 2026

Governor Adil pledges enormous support for establishment of reformatory centre through Kikiji National Orphanage Centre leadership

Juba Wednesday, 18th March 2026 CES-GPU

Governor Emmanuel Adil Anthony held a pivotal meeting with Dominic Living Wawa, the founder of the Kikiji National Orphanage, to advance plans for a major new social welfare project.

The meeting focused on the need for the establishment of the Juba Kikiji Rehabilitation Center, a dedicated facility aimed at protecting the city's most vulnerable children and sanctuary for orphans and underprivileged children.

Dominic Living Wawa expressed his deep gratitude to Governor Adil and the Juba County Commissioner for the successful allocation of land for the construction of the facility.

The Pledge

"An amount of SSP 50 Million (Fifty Million South Sudan Pounds) as the state's necessary support for its establishment."

The Governor emphasized that providing for vulnerable populations is a key priority for the state's social development and support to the needy population, especially the orphans across the State.
